How to interpret the results

Cash-secured puts: the maximum gain is the net option credit, while downside can be substantial if the shares collapse. Covered calls: you must own 100 deliverable shares per contract; upside is capped at the strike and stock downside remains. Long calls: the maximum loss is the premium plus fees, with potentially unlimited upside at expiration. Long puts: maximum loss is the premium plus fees, while upside is capped because the stock price cannot drop below zero. Early assignment, taxes, interest, slippage, bid-ask spreads, corporate actions, and changing implied volatility can affect real results. Fees are deducted, but the charts describe expiration P/L, not mark-to-market option prices. Underlying stock closes are historical. Theoretical option premiums are not tradable quotes. Use a broker to verify actual listed contracts, market bid/ask, liquidity and current expiration terms. Confirm contract size, liquidity, dividend timing, and current pricing with a broker.

ShareWatch Options Analyzer is an addition to the existing share-count and EPS tracking website. Calculations are illustrative, use standard 100-share non-adjusted U.S. equity option contracts, and do not constitute a trade recommendation. Neither high probability, premium yield, nor a positive assumed expected value guarantees profitability.
