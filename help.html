Start here

Three simple steps to get more out of ShareWatch.

1

Pick a stock

Open the Share Counter, search its ticker, then select the row to see share-count changes, SEC sources and the saved stock-price chart.

2

Explore options

Go to the Options Analyzer, choose a cash-secured put, covered call, long call or long put, then select an actual listed expiration.

3

Check your risk

Use Trading Tools to calculate premium, collateral, position size, average cost, dividends and growth scenarios. Verify any real trade with your broker.

You can browse ShareWatch without creating an account. Signing in is only needed for personal features like saving favorites and your private options trade journal. Administrator access is separate.

Frequently asked questions

Search for a question, or filter by subject. Select any question to read the answer.

Searchable guide

What is ShareWatch?

ShareWatch is a research dashboard that compares reported shares outstanding, shows saved stock-price history, and helps you analyze potential options trades with indicative quotes and educational calculations. It is not a brokerage and does not place orders.

How do I compare how much a company diluted its shares?

Use the Share Counter. Select a comparison period such as one year, then examine the percent change and the actual reporting dates. You can sort by largest increase or decrease. An increase in shares outstanding is a research flag, not definitive proof of a stock offering or shareholder dilution: splits, conversions, acquisitions and other corporate actions may affect the figure. Check the linked SEC filing.

What can I calculate in Trading Tools?

On the Trading Tools page you can estimate net cash-secured put or covered-call premium and yield, share position size based on planned risk, average share cost after buying more shares, dividend income, and hypothetical long-term compound growth. Tools use numbers you type and do not fetch live quotes or save your input.

What is the difference between cash-secured puts, covered calls, and buying an option?

Cash-secured put: you sell a put for premium and may be assigned 100 shares per standard contract at the strike, requiring cash collateral. Covered call: you sell a call against shares you already own and give up potential gains above its strike. Long call: you pay to gain bullish option exposure; the premium can be lost. Long put: you pay for bearish exposure; the premium can be lost. Confirm the actual contract multiplier because adjusted contracts may differ.

Are the Alpaca bid and ask prices real, executable quotes?

No. ShareWatch's Alpaca feed provides indicative modified prices, not an official exchange NBBO. The data may be delayed at least 15 minutes. A market order, limit fill or achievable premium cannot be inferred from it. Check your brokerage quote and available liquidity before trading.

What does “Profit chance (model est.)” mean?

It is an illustrative model-based estimate of the chance an option strategy has a positive expiration profit under simplifying assumptions involving current stock price, implied volatility and breakeven. It is not a historical win rate, not your broker's probability, and not guaranteed. A trade can have a high modeled win chance but still expose you to large losses. Missing inputs show —.

How can I see IV, bid/ask spread, quote sizes, delta and quote time?

In Options Analyzer → Step 2, strike and modeled profit chance are shown together so you don't need to scroll across a wide table. Select Details on a contract for additional measurements. ShareWatch does not claim quote sizes are daily trading volume or open interest.

How do I use the advanced options screener?

Go to the Advanced options screener. Choose short puts or covered calls, set maximum days to expiration, delta, spread and minimum premium, then scan a batch of up to six active tickers. Sort results by premium or return on collateral. The screener uses indicative quotes, and a high yield does not mean an option is safe or likely to profit.

What does annualized premium yield mean?

ShareWatch takes the premium return for the option period and scales it to a 365-day year. This is a simple comparison measure, not a predicted yearly return. You may not be able to find the same contract, price, volatility, or assignment outcome repeatedly. The Trading Tools calculator lets you adjust premium, days and fees.

Why does a stock show “No price history” or “Awaiting source”?

The stock may have been added after the last saved market-price collection, the provider may be unavailable, or there may be no supported historical observations. Stock prices and shares outstanding are collected separately. New stocks are checked by a five-minute scheduled workflow (which may start late), and the full price collector runs every six hours. Open its price chart and select Recheck saved prices if that option appears. This reloads published data; it does not trigger a new market-data download.

Why are share counts only recorded a few times per year?

Companies usually disclose shares outstanding through periodic SEC reports, while many ETFs report shares in other ways. These observations have their own report dates and are different from daily stock prices. The chart deliberately does not invent daily share counts between filings.

Why is an earnings or dividend date missing?

The calendar only shows data returned and validated from its source feed. Some tickers, ETFs or events may have no entries, and event dates can change. Historical EPS markers on a stock price chart come from SEC filings and are not a guaranteed future earnings schedule. Confirm event dates on the issuer's investor-relations website before trading.

How often does ShareWatch refresh?

Full share-count and saved closing-price updates are scheduled about every six hours; missing data for newly added tickers is checked by a separate workflow scheduled every five minutes. The calendar has a daily schedule. The Alpaca option chain loads on request and has a roughly five-minute browser cache and a server cache up to 90 seconds. GitHub Actions and market-data vendors may delay or fail updates; the timestamps on each section are more informative than the cron schedule alone.

Do I need an account? How do I save my favorite tickers?

Browsing prices, shares and options is public. Select Account on the dashboard (or Sign in / Create account on Options Analyzer), sign in, then select the star beside a ticker. The ★ My favorites filter shows your saved list. Favorites belong to your account.

Is my Options Portfolio tracker linked to my brokerage account?

No. The Options portfolio tracker is a private, manually entered trade journal. Only signed-in users can access their own saved rows through Supabase row-level permissions. Realized option P/L depends on the opening premium, closing premium and fees you enter; it excludes gains or losses on stock assignments, exercise, taxes, slippage and margin costs.

Can everyone use the Admin Portal?

No. The Admin Portal requires an account that has been granted administrator permissions. A normal account can view the public Share Counter and Options Analyzer but cannot add, hide or delete tickers from the shared watchlist.

The stock price or options data looks old. What should I do?

Check the visible source timestamps and whether the price is a historical closing price or an indicative options snapshot. On Options Analyzer, select Refresh option chain to request an updated result; backend caching may still apply. For share and price collectors, review GitHub Actions and the published timestamp. Refreshing the page does not force GitHub to run its collectors immediately.

Why are some options not listed, or why is an expiration missing?

Alpaca may return only a partial contract catalog, no listed contracts for that ticker, unavailable option snapshots, or a temporarily inaccessible feed. Some stocks and funds simply have no listed options. Try a different expiration, check your connection and the quote status, and verify the complete chain with your broker.

Where can I find official stock filings and learn about options risk?

See SEC EDGAR company filings ↗, Investor.gov ↗, and the Options Industry Council ↗. These are separate sites and are not endorsements of any trading strategy.

What is the new Market Research Center?

The Market Research Center adds five features: indicative income opportunities, wheel and roll planners, dated SEC financial data, dividend coverage checks and protective-put/collar scenarios. Each section labels the origin and freshness of its numbers.

How does the Daily Income Opportunities scanner work?

The Income Opportunities scanner requests Alpaca indicative option snapshots for up to six active tickers and at most two nearby listed expirations per ticker. It shows OTM contracts meeting your premium, delta and spread filters. Premium yield uses collateral or saved stock close, and simple annualized yield is not a predicted annual return. This is an on-demand scanner, not an automatically published daily trade recommendation; modified quotes may not be executable.

How should I interpret the Wheel and Roll Planner?

The Wheel Planner models the separate possible stages of selling a cash-secured put and, only if assigned shares, potentially selling a covered call. The Roll Planner compares premiums paid to close with premiums received when opening the new contract. Rolling does not erase the old option's loss, and the assumed future covered call or assignment is not guaranteed.

Why do some SEC financial or dividend figures say unavailable?

The Market Intelligence Center draws from source-labeled SEC companyfacts collected in batches. Different issuers report different financial concepts; newly added tickers may not have been processed yet. Annual earnings, revenue and cash flows can also refer to different fiscal years from balance-sheet values. Missing metrics are deliberately not estimated.

Does ShareWatch rate which dividends are safe?

No. The Dividend Safety Center provides historical SEC annual EPS, reported dividend-per-share when disclosed, operating cash flow, same-period free cash flow and a payout proxy based on the annual dividend you enter. It does not verify that your entered dividend is a currently declared rate, nor can any single historical ratio guarantee that a dividend will continue.

What does the Risk and Hedge Center calculate?

It shows expiration outcomes for an existing stock position with a protective put or a collar (long put and short covered call). The put can provide a floor at expiration, while the collar limits potential gains above its call strike. Calculations use manual premiums, standard 100-share contracts and simplified expiration payoffs; dividends, early assignment and trading execution costs may affect actual results.

What is the Market Data Lab?

Open Market Data Lab for five research tools: historical OHLCV candlesticks, SEC Form 4 insider filings, manual official short-interest comparisons, explainable stock signals and SEC-based valuation ratios. The page uses your existing active stock watchlist and does not place orders.

Why are some candlestick charts or volume signals unavailable?

Choose any tracked stock in Advanced Stock Charts. The collector is scheduled four times daily and now checks the full active watchlist, rather than only 16 tickers per run. If source-verified daily OHLCV hasn't been collected for a ticker yet, ShareWatch automatically shows its existing historical closing-price observations as a line chart, marked as weekly or irregularly sampled where appropriate. It never invents candles, trading volume, SMA or RSI from those observations. Select Reload chart data after the scheduled collection to see new source bars. Providers may block downloads or not cover a particular symbol; all data is historical, not live.

How are insider purchases and sales verified?

The Insider Activity section links to SEC Form 4 filings. Only non-derivative transaction codes P (purchase) and S (sale) parsed from an SEC ownership document or official structured data are classified. Form 4 may also report grants, exercises and transfers. Unverified filings are shown as unclassified, and missing filings do not imply that no insider trading occurred. SEC quarterly data arrives with a publication lag.

Does the Short Interest Tracker automatically download short interest?

Yes, when FINRA provides a validated report. The Short Interest Tracker automatically reads dated historical short-position counts from a scheduled collector using the FINRA public consolidatedShortInterest API, with recent settlement dates and reported days-to-cover metrics. No manual entry is needed. It is not a live quote: official short positions are published periodically, and an unavailable ticker stays blank rather than being replaced with daily short-sale volume. Daily short-sale volume is not outstanding short interest. Public-feed coverage and redistribution terms may change.

Are Smart Stock Signals predictions or buy recommendations?

No. The Smart Stock Signals section uses historical price, volume and share-count data to flag explainable conditions, such as a close above 20 previous session highs, relative volume of at least twice its recent average, moving-average positioning and RSI thresholds. Signals can be misleading or fail after corporate actions. They are research prompts, not trade recommendations or expected returns.

How are valuation ratios calculated?

The Valuation Center automatically uses SEC XBRL company facts, reported shares outstanding and saved historical stock closes. Market cap requires a share count from the last approximately 18 months. P/E uses annual SEC diluted EPS, or clearly identified basic EPS when diluted is unavailable. P/S uses annual reported revenue. P/B prefers directly reported stockholders equity and otherwise derives equity from assets minus liabilities in a matching period. Net margin requires same-period annual net income and revenue. Free cash flow requires same-period cash flow and capital expenditures; otherwise the card shows operating cash flow with a different label, not an invented FCF amount.

Each card says why a value is missing. An unavailable figure may mean the stock is pending collection, the filing has no comparable concept, the source is too old, or the figure is undefined (for example negative equity or EPS). Funds and some foreign issuers require different valuation methods. SEC results are historical and do not supply a guaranteed fair-value price.
